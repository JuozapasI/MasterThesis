In the results chapter, we demonstrated that unassigned reads constitute a significant fraction of scRNA-seq data.
These reads can be broadly divided into two categories: those that overlap with annotated genes and those that do not.

Among the non-overlapping reads, we distinguished two subtypes: antisense reads, located on the opposite strand of known genes,
and isolated reads, which do not lie near any annotated genes.
Antisense regions comprised the majority of defined intergenic regions.
Based on observed correlations and the presence of poly-A stretches,
we hypothesize that many antisense reads are artifacts generated during the reverse transcription reaction.

In contrast, isolated reads may represent unannotated genes,
particularly when supported by additional evidence such as differential expression or ATAC-seq signals.
However, the short-read 3' end sequencing protocols employed in this study only allow us to localize transcriptional activity,
not to reconstruct gene structures.
Verification of these novel genes should therefore rely on methods capable of capturing full-length RNA molecules.

To reduce reverse transcription artifacts, lowering the reaction temperature may be beneficial,
as RNA–cDNA hybrids are more stable at lower temperatures.
In the 10x Genomics v3.1 protocol, reverse transcription is performed at $52^\circ C$,
while in inDrops2 it occurs at $42^\circ C$, where we observed fewer unassigned reads (see Appendix \ref{tab:countStats}).

For reads overlapping with known genes, the predominant cause of unassignment is gene overlap.
When a read maps to a region shared by two or more genes, assignment becomes ambiguous.
Our proposed pipeline, which resolves such overlaps based on gene type, yielded an average increase of $\sim 1.2\%$ in cell-gene matrix counts.
While the overall number of assigned reads and detected genes increased, some genes showed reduced counts after overlap resolution.
This highlights the complexity of the transcriptome and the challenges of disambiguating gene overlaps using short-read data.

Several alternative strategies exist for handling multimapped reads, including uniform distribution,
probabilistic assignment based on uniquely mapped read ratios, and others \cite{Francoeur2020}.
However, no consensus or gold standard currently exists, and to minimize uncertainty, such reads are often excluded from downstream analyses.

A key limitation of our overlap resolution strategy is its gene-level granularity: it relies solely on gene start and end coordinates.
In contrast, transcript annotations include exon–intron structures and multiple isoforms.
A tool that accounts for these finer-resolution features — such as exon boundaries and transcript structures —
could potentially recover more reads and improve assignment precision.

It is important to emphasize that the datasets used in this study were generated with 3' short-read protocols.
Artifacts observed here may differ in datasets obtained using other methods.
Nevertheless, the candidate novel genes identified remain valid and merit further investigation.
Lastly, this study focused exclusively on uniquely mapped unassigned reads, leaving another subset of unassigned reads unexplored in this study.
