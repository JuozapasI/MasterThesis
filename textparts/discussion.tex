In the results chapter was shown that the unassigned reads constitute significant part of total scRNAseq data.
Such reads could roughly be categorized into two classes: overlapping with genes in the genome annotation and not.

For those overlapping with known genes, the main reason for the being unassigned is ovelapping genes
(i.e. if a read maps to the region where two genes are present, mapping algorith does not know, for which to assign).
The suggested pipeline to resolve these overlaps (mainly based on gene type) allowed to have up to ????\% more genes in
the downstream analysis.

Those that are not overlapping with genes, where classified either as antisense
(located on the opposite strand of the known genes) or isolated (remaining ones).
The observations about correlations and poly-A stretches allowed us to make hypothesis
that those reads are artifacts generated during reverse transcription reaction.
Isolated reads, on the other hand, can originate from unannotated genes, especially those,
that were supported by some additional evidence, such as differential expression or ATAC-seq data.

It should be noted, that datasets used were generated using \textbf{short-read 3' end} protocols.
Therefore, datasets prepared using different methods might have different artifacts present.
However, the new gene candidates remain valid, and they should be verified by some additional experiments.

As for the reverse transcription reaction artefacts, it is worth considering performing this reaction in lower temperatures,
as RNA-cDNA complex is more stable in such temperatures.
