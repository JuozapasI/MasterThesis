In the results chapter, it was shown that unassigned reads constitute a significant portion of total scRNA-seq data.
These reads can be broadly categorized into two groups: those overlapping with annotated genes and those that do not.

The non-overlapping reads were clustered and classified as either antisense (located on the opposite strand of known genes)
or isolated (remaining ones).
Antisense regions constituted the vast majority of defined intergenic regions.
Observations regarding correlations and poly-A stretches led us to hypothesize
that these antisense reads are artifacts generated during the reverse transcription reaction.
Isolated reads, on the other hand, may originate from unannotated genes,
particularly those supported by additional evidence such as differential expression or ATAC-seq data.

To mitigate artifacts arising from the reverse transcription reaction, it may be beneficial to conduct this reaction at lower temperatures,
as the RNA-cDNA complex is more stable under such conditions.
In the 10x Genomics v3.1 protocol, this reaction occurs at $52^\circ C$, whereas in inDrops2, it is performed at $42^\circ C$,
where lower levels of unassigned reads are observed.

For reads overlapping with known genes, the primary reason for being unassigned is gene overlap.
When a read maps to a region where two genes are present, the mapping algorithm cannot confidently assign it to a single gene.
The proposed pipeline for resolving these overlaps, primarily based on gene type,
resulted in on average approximately 1.2\% more counts in the cell-gene matrices.
However, while the total number of assigned reads and captured genes increased, some genes exhibited decreased total counts after overlap resolution.
This underscores the complexity of the human genome and the challenges of resolving overlaps when mapping short reads.

Several alternative approaches exist for handling multimappers, such as uniform distribution,
assignment based on uniquely mapped read ratios, and others (\cite{Francoeur2020}).
However, none of these methods are universally accepted as gold standards,
and in most cases, to minimize uncertainty, such reads are excluded from downstream analysis.

The main limitation of the proposed method for resolving gene overlaps is that it operates at the gene level,
meaning only the start and end coordinates of genes are considered.
However, transcriptomic annotations are typically more detailed, containing information about different transcripts, exons, and other features.
Therefore, creating a tool that considers transcript and exon structures, rather than just gene start and end coordinates,
would likely yield better results in terms of both the amount of data recovered and precision.

It is important to note that the datasets used in this study were generated using short-read 3' end protocols.
Datasets prepared with different methods may exhibit distinct artifacts.
However, the new gene candidates identified in this study remain valid and should be verified through additional experiments.
Additionally, only uniquely mapped unassigned reads were analyzed, leaving another subset of unassigned reads unexplored in this study.
